Abstract—This proposal describes a controlled empirical evaluation of tools intended to reduce energy consumption in Android applications. We examine static analysis (Android Lint) and dynamic profiling (Android Studio Profiler) across 30 open-source applications and six physical devices. Baseline and refactored versions undergo paired energy measurements to determine practical effectiveness.

Keywords—Android, energy consumption, software engineering, refactoring, power profiling, continuous integration.

I. INTRODUCTION

Energy optimization in Android applications requires empirical evidence that a code modification lowers battery drain during active execution without compromising functionality. Prior studies by Palomba et al. examined code smells in Android apps, demonstrating relationships between code architecture and battery life [1].

The primary contribution of this study is a reproducible measurement protocol evaluating energy alongside functional correctness, latency, developer effort, and CI deployment feasibility.

II. MATHEMATICAL FORMULATION

Total energy consumption \(E_{\text{total}}\) is calculated by integrating instantaneous bus voltage \(V(t)\) and current draw \(I(t)\) across the benchmark workload duration \(T\):

Etotal = ∫0T V(t) · I(t) dt (1)

The relative percentage energy savings \(\Delta E\) between the baseline and refactored versions is formulated as:

ΔE = [(Ebaseline − Erefactored) / Ebaseline] × 100 (2)

IV. CONCLUSION

This study outlines an empirical benchmark protocol for Android software energy refactoring. By combining static analysis and dynamic profiling, developers can identify actionable optimizations while verifying behavioral correctness.
